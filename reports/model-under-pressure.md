# Model Under Pressure Replication Report

See also: [Generalization analysis](generalization.md) — taxonomy of why this method failed to transfer.

This project replicates and extends the paper on detecting high-stakes LLM interactions with activation probes. We reproduced the Llama-3.3-70B@L31 attention and softmax baselines, matching the paper’s reported performance closely, then tested the same probes across Qwen3-32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, and GLM-4-32B at multiple layers. The main takeaway is that probe performance is partly reproducible, but transfer across models and layers is inconsistent, with some extensions holding up and others dropping sharply.

## Summary

Paper: arXiv 2506.10805 (Llama-3.3-70B @ L31). "Replicates well?" = baseline replication falls in the paper's reported band. "Generalizes well?" = mean AUROC within 0.02 of our baseline replication (attention 0.9184, softmax 0.9244), all on the six balanced test datasets.

### Data Audit

The probe is trained on synthetic examples generated and judged by GPT-4o. The evaluation artifacts combine human conversations and prompts (Anthropic HH-RLHF), social-media statements (Mental Health), medical reports (MT Samples) and doctor–patient dialogues (MTS Dialog), red-team prompts (Aya Redteaming), and third-party synthetic tool-use traces (ToolACE). Anthropic HH-RLHF and ToolACE contain traces from other models, not trajectories generated by the monitored Llama or Qwen model. The paper explicitly frames these as out-of-distribution deployment data: samples are scored by GPT-4o on a 1–10 stakes scale with confidence, ambiguous cases are removed, and the remainder is balanced 50/50 into development and test sets. This therefore tests synthetic-to-external, domain, and context transfer.

The audit also found reproducibility caveats. The generator's `high_stakes` flag and final GPT-4o label disagree on roughly 4–5% of released training rows; the paper's C.3/C.4 confidence cutoffs are inconsistent; the paper lists 1,513 Aya test examples while the current balanced artifact contains 1,242; and balanced selection uses unseeded `random.sample`, so regeneration can select different rows. The paper itself attributes errors to context noise in Mental Health, deployment-context dependence in ToolACE, and culturally nuanced Aya examples (the weakest GPT-4o and manual agreement with 75%). These findings support a provenance and context, and label-shift hypothesis.

We used the authors' released modified HF artifacts for the synthetic set (8,000 train/2,000 test), Anthropic HH-RLHF (1,028/2,984), ToolACE (328/734), MT Samples (278/604), MTS Dialog (274/86), and Mental Health (540 test); counts match the paper. These are not untouched source datasets: they were GPT-4o relabeled, filtered, balanced, and sometimes system/context-transformed. Aya Redteaming is not exact: the paper reports 1,513 test examples, while our HF artifact has 1,242. Anthropic also uses HH-RLHF’s train split. ### Reading the full metric suite

Metrics pulled from `r2://mup-replication/results/llama70b_layer31_softmax.jsonl` and tabulated above.

**Reading the table.** Ranking quality is uniformly strong (AUROC 0.835–0.991; AUPRC tracks it), but the fixed 0.5 threshold is badly miscalibrated _per dataset_: `toolace` collapses to 0.038 recall (the probe rarely crosses 0.5 there), while `mental_health` sits at 0.50 accuracy / 0.97 recall / 0.0 MCC (predicting almost everything positive). `mts` shows TPR@1%FPR = 0.0 because n=86 makes the 1%-FPR operating point unreachable. These are calibration artifacts of a single global threshold, not ranking failures — a per-dataset or per-family threshold would recover the point-wise metrics. This is itself a generalization-relevant finding: the _decision boundary_ transfers far worse than the _ordering_, which matters for any deployment that fixes one threshold across use cases.

**Softmax loses to attention at both Qwen layers.** Probe-swap Δ (softmax − attention): L32 = 0.8206 − 0.858 = **−0.037**; L47 = 0.7667 − 0.8812 = **−0.114**. So on Qwen the softmax head consistently underperforms attention, and the gap widens with depth. `mental_health` is the swing construct: fine at L32 (0.879) but collapsed to chance at L47 (0.500) under softmax — a depth-localized failure of the softmax objective for that concept on this family.

**Probe-controlled cross-family comparison.** At the _same_ layer (Qwen3-32B L47), swapping attention → softmax drops mean AUROC from **0.8812 → 0.7667** (−0.114). Contrast Llama-3.3-70B L31, where the same swap _raised_ AUROC (0.9184 → 0.9244, +0.006). So the two probe heads are interchangeable on Llama but not on Qwen — the softmax head loses ~0.11 AUROC when moved off the family it was validated on. This is the probe-controlled evidence the generalization thesis needs: the cross-family degradation is not a fixed property of one probe architecture (attention holds up on Qwen far better than softmax does), which points at an interaction between probe inductive bias and how the concept is represented in each family, rather than at probe design alone. `mental_health` collapses to chance (AUROC 0.500) under softmax on Qwen — worse than its already-weak 0.639 attention score — flagging it as the least transferable construct. TPR@1%FPR is 0.0 across every Qwen dataset, underscoring that the miscalibration seen on Llama is far more severe off-family.

## July 3, 2026 | Paper deep-read + three-way comparison (paper vs replication vs extension)

Read arXiv 2506.10805v4 in full (main text + appendices A–D) to fix the baseline column of the comparison. Key setup extracted from the source:

**Probe architectures (paper §2.1, Table with f_θ definitions).** Let `A = [a_1…a_S] ∈ ℝ^{S×D}` be residual activations before the layer-norm of a chosen layer. Probe output is `p_θ(A) = σ(f_θ(A))`.

| Probe                | f_θ(A)                                                          |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mean                 | `(1/                                                            |
| Max                  | `max{ θᵀ a_1, …, θᵀ a_S }`                                      |
| Last Token           | `θᵀ a_S`                                                        |
| Max of Rolling Means | `max over windows of size T of the rolling mean of θᵀ a` (T=40) |
| Softmax              | `softmax(Aθ/φ)ᵀ Aθ` (temperature φ=5)                           |
| Attention            | `softmax(Aθ_q)ᵀ Aθ_v`, θ = {θ_q, θ_v}                           |

**Training hyperparameters (paper §A.1).** Attention probe: 200 epochs, early-stop 50, LR 5e-3 → 5e-4, batch 16 (128 for the dev-training experiment), grad-accum 4.
